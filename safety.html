River safety guidance

For the Río Lérez swim visualisation. This is general planning context, not an official event notice, a go/no-go decision, or a substitute for the on-site Technical Delegate, medical team, and safety briefing.

High-flow is a caution condition

A modeled mid-channel speed is not a river-safety clearance. In a constricted tidal river, velocity can vary across the channel and around bends, bridge structures, shallow margins, and obstructions. The displayed values are planning estimates, not an on-water current survey.

For athletes

  • Follow the official briefing, marshals, buoyed route, extraction instructions, and any race modification or cancellation without exception.
  • Use only the clear, buoyed course line. Do not chase a near-bank line around bridge structures, shallow water, obstacles, or visible eddies.
  • Where the buoyed line is clear and officials permit it, slower near-bank water may reduce exposure to faster mid-channel flow. It is a tactical consideration, not a safety guarantee.
  • Do not apply this page as advice for unsupervised river swimming. Trained safety cover, controlled access/exit, and a documented rescue plan are essential parts of an organised event.

Why the World Triathlon threshold is not the whole answer

World Triathlon permits a Technical Delegate to modify or cancel a swim for current or other environmental safety concerns. Its rule also requires rescheduling or cancellation for Age Group and Para athletes swimming against current above 0.5 m/s, measured close to the bank. That is an event-rule threshold; it does not make a different current measurement elsewhere in the channel safe.
